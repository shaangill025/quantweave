## Changes (4 code files + this receipt)
- **`qw_domain/option_risk.py`**
  - **`contract_terms`** reduces a T011 `OptionContract` to N underlying units plus cash C, against a strike payment K×M per contract. The effective strike is K′ = (K×M − C)/N. Nothing defaults to 100.
    - Terms that are unverified (including adjusted terms not yet verified) or unknown return `Unavailable`. The code is the first reason from `sizing_block_reasons`, and the reason text lists all of them. Unknown terms include the multiplier, the deliverables, cash in lieu, the exercise style and the settlement.
    - A deliverable without units of the underlying (for example cash only), baskets, cash in a foreign currency, a negative cash component and a non-positive K′ also return `Unavailable`.
  - **`expiry_intrinsic`** follows the spec 09 payoff convention for each long contract. Adjusted deliverables use the reduction above.
  - **`check_cover`** checks the open state, netted by contract, for one account and one currency (R043: another currency returns `Unavailable`). It applies the fully secured convention from spec 09:
    - Premium paid is spent. Premium received never funds anything, because it is unsettled.
    - A short put reserves the gross K×M.
    - A short call encumbers N held units and also reserves the cash component C. Units cover only one call. A cash-settled short call is uncovered.
    - A short paired with a long of the same right, expiry and terms reserves the width of the strike payment. The long must be at least as exercisable as the short: an American long covers either style, and a European long covers only a European short. Other pairs stay unpaired.
    - Among admissible pairings, greedy pairing can over-reserve the expiry loss but never under-reserve it.
    - Premiums are counted per `check_cover` call. Every paid premium among the legs is spent, and premium received on closing legs is not netted. This is conservative.
    - `AccountCover.units` is not reduced by other encumbrances, such as other proposals or structures. The caller must pass units that are already net of them.
    - Broker permissions come from the caller. Unknown or missing permissions block.
    - Broker margin formulas are not invented.
  - **`check_legging`** checks every intermediate state, including from existing legs, and blocks if any state blocks. It reports the worst state.
  - **`vertical_bounds`** gives the idealized max loss and max gain from spec 09, including fees.
    - Debit or credit comes from the structure, not from the premium sign. A call spread long the lower strike, or a put spread long the higher strike, is a debit structure.
    - Max loss and max gain then use the actual net premium.
    - Premiums and fees must be in the strike currency and fees must be ≥ 0; otherwise it returns a typed `Unavailable`.
    - Costs round up and gains round down.
- **`qw_domain/decimal_math.py`** provides `exp`, `ln` and `sqrt`, using `decimal`'s correctly rounded operations at 40 digits.
  - `norm_cdf` uses the Φ series with guard digits that grow with x². For |x| ≥ 15 it returns exactly 0 or 1, since the tail there is below 4e-51.
  - The stated absolute tolerance is 1e-35.
  - `exp` underflows to 0 below about x = −2.3e6, because Underflow is not trapped. This is tested.
  - Division goes through an explicit context `divide`; `check_no_float` bans the `/` operator.
- Tests: `test_option_risk.py` has 12 tests (22 cases) and `test_decimal_math.py` has 4 tests (10 cases).

## Negative and adverse cases
- Missing multiplier or deliverables, unknown style, and adjusted terms that are not verified each return `Unavailable` and are never sized.
- Two short calls on 100 units are uncovered, and the adjusted call needs all 150 units. A cash shortfall of USD 0.01 on the cash deliverable or the combined put reserve blocks.
- Unknown and missing broker permissions block.
- Cross-currency cash returns `Unavailable`.
- Inputs are rejected for:
  - a float or zero leg quantity;
  - a float into the Decimal functions;
  - ln(0) and sqrt(−1).
- Legging:
  - A bull put credit entered short first blocks at state 0 (5000 against 1000), while the long-first order passes.
  - A bear call credit entered short first is uncovered.
  - Closing the long leg first leaves a naked short and blocks. Buying back the short first passes.
- Vertical bounds:
  - A bear call paid at a net debit of 400 (width 500) gives loss 900 and gain −400.
  - A bull call at a net credit of 400 gives loss −400 and gain 900.
  - A zero-premium bull call loses the fee only.
  - A fee or premium in another currency, or a negative fee, is refused.
- A European long does not cover an American short, so the short reserves the full 5000. An American long does cover a European short.
- A negative cash deliverable and a cash-only deliverable fail closed.
