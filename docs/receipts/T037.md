### Rules
- **One book per scope and UTC calendar month.** The spec names no budget timezone, so UTC applies (spec §3). A reservation timed in another month is refused (`wrong_period`).
- **Caps.** Every category draws on the inference cap. Improvement also has its own cap inside it, so USD20 of improvement counts toward the USD100 (§11). Committed = open reservations + spent, where spent is the actual for settled reservations and the full reservation for expired ones. F-18: committed ≤ cap, for the whole book and for improvement.
- **Retries.** A retry with the same id and the same terms returns the book unchanged (no new `created_at` or deadline), but only while the reservation is still reserved and not dispatched. A released, expired, settled or dispatched reservation is refused (`reservation_closed`). The same id with different terms is refused (`duplicate_reservation`).
- **Dispatch.** `dispatched(id, at)` is refused at or after the reservation deadline (`deadline_passed`) and once the owner authorization ref is no longer set (`spending_not_authorized`).
- **Settling.** `settle` needs a dispatched reservation (or an expired one) and a report time no earlier than the reservation (`report_before_reservation`). It records the reported actual, which releases the remainder. The charge always stays in the reservation's month, even when the report arrives in a later month (tested across 2026-10/2026-11). An actual above the reservation is recorded as reported, not truncated. It makes `available` negative and blocks new reservations.
- **Releasing and expiry.** `release` works only on a reservation that was never dispatched. A dispatched reservation is never released: it stays reserved until a usage report arrives or its deadline passes. `expire_due` then charges it at the full reservation (status `expired`), and a later report can still settle it. Undispatched reservations past their deadline are released.
- **Atomicity.** The book is immutable and carries a `version`. `apply(current, new, expected_version)` installs a new book only if `current` is still at `expected_version`; otherwise it raises `stale_version`. Persistence must do the same in one transaction (`UPDATE ... WHERE version = expected`). The in-memory test shows the protocol, not database atomicity: 20 threads wait on a `threading.Barrier` so all compute from the same book, write through `apply` under a lock, and retry on `stale_version`. The test asserts at least one compare-and-set failure. A control that writes without the version check loses updates (more grants than reservations in the final book).
- **Late reports and authentication.** A late usage report overwrites the worst-case charge of an expired reservation. The gateway must therefore authenticate the reporter (only the provider adapter for the admitted route) before calling `settle`.
- **Rounding.** The book takes amounts as given; rounding cost up to the micro-dollar is the metering code's job (gateway increment).

## Negative cases (SYNTHETIC)
- **Caps.** A reservation one micro-dollar over the inference cap is refused, and the book is unchanged. The improvement cap is enforced, and improvement exhaustion still leaves monitoring available (AT032). Improvement is also blocked by the inference cap.
- **Configuration.** Spending is refused without an owner authorization. Bad config is rejected: a JSON number, improvement above inference, a negative cap, or a missing key.
- **Settling and expiry.**
  - Settlement is final.
  - An overrun is recorded and blocks further reservations.
  - A dispatched reservation cannot be released.
  - Nothing expires one microsecond before the deadline. At the deadline the call is charged at the worst case, and a late report corrects the charge.
  - An undispatched reservation cannot be settled.
- **Inputs.** The UTC month boundary is applied to a −05:00 local time. Naive datetimes, float money, Decimal passed in place of `UsdBudget`, and zero or negative amounts are all rejected.
- **Concurrency.** 20 barrier-synchronised threads race for USD7 each against USD100 through `apply`. At least one compare-and-set conflict occurs; exactly 14 are granted, for USD98 in total, and the final book holds all 14. Without the version check (control) updates are lost. `apply` refuses stale versions.
- **Closed reservations.** Reviewer probes: reserve → release → re-reserve, and dispatch → expire → re-reserve, are both refused (`reservation_closed`); so are dispatched and settled ids.
- **Dispatch guards.** Dispatch at the deadline or after the authorization is removed is refused; one microsecond before the deadline it succeeds.
- **Property.** Under random reserve, dispatch, settle, release and expire sequences, committed never exceeds either cap and no actual exceeds its reservation. This holds only because the property reports actuals at or below the reservation; a real overrun is recorded as reported and can take committed above a cap (tested separately).
- **Oracles.** Expected amounts are hand-computed.

## Rules
- **Fail closed (T006 unresolved).** A `Route` built without qualification is `unqualified` with no rates; `qualified` needs a `qualification_ref`; zero-priced rates are rejected (config: never imply an unmetered API). No route config exists, so every real call is refused (`no_route` / `no_qualified_route`).
- **Admission order.** Rights first and route-independent: tenant AI consent for `tenant_financial` data and `model_processing` rights for every feed; a denial is never routed around. A declared `data=none` is a caller assertion: it skips consent and the route's financial-use, training and retention checks, but feed rights are still checked. Per route: qualification, rates, purpose, data class, financial use (R095), training opt-out and retention terms for tenant data, tools, input/output tokens, wall time, attempts. All failures are listed as `Denial(reason, detail)`.
- **Budget.** Worst case = attempts x (input x input rate + output x output rate) / 1e6, rounded up (`Rounding.COST`) to USD0.000001, reserved through `BudgetBook.reserve` with deadline = admission + attempts x wall + grace. Budget refusals are typed `budget:<code>`.
- **Fallback.** A route after the first needs a `FallbackPermission` that names it; it passes the same checks and must not weaken the primary's data class, financial-use, training or retention terms (`fallback_privacy_downgrade`). The permission ref is recorded on the admission and the run.
- **Dispatch.** `begin` starts attempt n through `BudgetBook.begin_send` (refused while one is in flight, after `attempts`, or unless reserved, authorized and before the deadline). It returns the book and a `DispatchTicket` bound to that book's version; only `begin` can issue one (`forged_ticket`). `dispatch` refuses before any adapter call (`not_begun`) unless the ticket is unused and the book is that version, and refuses at or past the deadline (`deadline_passed`; the attempt stays open for `expire_due`). The adapter gets send time + wall, capped at the deadline. An adapter error or a refused settlement (gateway or book, e.g. `report_before_reservation`) returns the attempt via `end_send`, keeps the reservation and records outcome `unknown`. **What is guaranteed:** one call per ticket in-process. **PRECONDITION:** the caller installs `begin`'s book by compare-and-set, and at most once across processes needs persistence to hold the begun attempt as an exclusive claim (row lock or lease) across the provider call; the domain cannot detect a replayed post-begin book.
- **Retries are charged conservatively (§10).** A settlement charges the reported cost plus, for every other begun attempt (outcome unknown), the per-attempt worst case recorded at admission. Usage above the request bounds is billed as reported and flagged `usage_over_bounds`.
- **Reporter authentication (precondition).** `meter` settles only a report with an `auth_ref`, from the admitted route's provider account, for the admitted reservation. The domain cannot verify the authentication itself; the adapter must set `auth_ref` only after verifying the channel.
- **Structured output.** The response must be a JSON object with exactly the declared fields and kinds; floats, NaN, duplicate keys, extra or missing fields are rejected. An invalid output is billed (settled) but never returned (`output_invalid`).
- **Provenance.** `ModelRun` follows `model_run`: route, provider account, model requested/returned, runtime version, prompt SHA-256, input-manifest hash, output SHA-256, tokens, cost, fallback ref, dispatched/finished times (UTC), outcome. It holds no prompt or response text.

# Increment 3: the durable budget book (owner decision 94 prerequisite)

Status: In progress. Persistence for `qw_domain.budget.BudgetBook`, with no dependency, provider, credential or billable call. Wiring the optimizer verdict to the book (decision 94) is the next increment.

**Changes.** `0052_budget_book.sql` (new): `app.budget_authorization` (only the owner, qw_migrate, writes it) and `app.budget_book` (one row per tenant and UTC month), both with forced RLS, plus an invoker guard using the helpers `budget_book_id`, `budget_book_instant` and `budget_book_step`. `budget_store.py` (new): `load`, `open_book`, `authorize`, `install` (compare-and-set) and `expire_due`. `budget.py`: `MAX_DEADLINE` (7 days); `reserve` now refuses a later deadline (`deadline_too_far`). Tests: `test_budget_store.py` (new); `test_budget.py`; a gateway-level `deadline_too_far` case in `test_gateway.py`; and the `test_tenancy.py` table inventory.

**Scope.** `scope_id` is the tenant id, giving one book (one cap pool) per tenant and month, because the gateway requires `req.tenant_id == book.scope_id`. Decision 94 must map the optimizer's installation scope (`improvement.py`: `book.scope_id == p.scope_id`) to the operator tenant's book. I rejected an installation-scoped book: the database has no record of the installation id, and a second pool would double the USD20 cap.

**Caps and authorization.** A CHECK binds the caps to `budgets_and_slos.json` (USD100 / USD20). `authorization_ref` must be null or a ref the owner recorded for that tenant, using direct SQL (`SET ROLE qw_migrate; INSERT INTO app.budget_authorization ...`); there is no API. The only caps change allowed is null → a recorded ref, made by `authorize`, or by `open_book` when that is the only difference. Revoking a ref takes effect once the owner's DELETE commits, because the trigger reads the table in its statement snapshot. After that, new spending is blocked but settles still go through. A revoked ref A cannot be switched to a newly recorded ref B, so that tenant's book takes no new spend until the next UTC month. That is a gap in the owner path. An authorization row has no scope or expiry. This follows decision 85's "bind the grant" by analogy only: decision 85 concerns research rights, and no owner decision covers this table.

**Roles.** qw_app and qw_worker read, open and update books; nobody deletes. qw_worker serves the gateway, the sweep and the optimizer cycle job (decisions 87, 92, 93, 97). qw_assessor, qw_release, qw_ingest and PUBLIC hold nothing.

**Guard.** It applies to direct SQL by those roles, and so to the store. At the database clock, it accepts only what `BudgetBook.from_wire` can load and what a `budget.py` step makes: Insert: an opened book for the current month only. Every update raises the version by exactly 1. Immutable fields: tenant, scope, period and caps. No reservation may be dropped or duplicated. Wire: each reservation must be the exact canonical form: ASCII `ID_PATTERN` ids, plain decimals with reserved > 0, and instants exactly as `format_instant` writes them. Transitions: dispatch and begin_send only before the deadline, while authorized, one send at a time; end_send only for a send in flight; release only if never dispatched; expire only at or after the deadline, with actual = reserved; settle only once dispatched. Settled and released are terminal. Additions: at most one per write, freshly reserved and authorized, created in the last 60 s (else `stale_clock`, which is retryable), with a deadline at most `MAX_DEADLINE` out, and within both caps summed over the book.

**Overrun trade-off.** A reported actual is kept as reported, however large: it may leave the book above a cap, and it then blocks additions. Round 2 had refused any actual above the inference cap. Round 3 removed that, because the gateway (`gateway.py:568`) then returned the attempt with the reservation still open: `expire_due` charged only the reserved amount, so real spend was under-recorded and new reservations were not blocked. An absurd report now locks that tenant's book for the month. This fails closed and loses no spend record. Gap: there is no owner path to correct a bad usage report.

**Deadline bound.** The 7-day bound is my choice; no config limits wall time, attempts or grace (the gateway's `Bounds` are per route). The domain refuses a longer deadline, so gateway admission returns a typed `Reason.BUDGET` / `deadline_too_far` denial (tested at the gateway). Improvement scheduling (`improvement.py:746`, deadline = at + max_duration) now defers such an experiment with `deadline_too_far`. A test pins 0052 to the same constant.

**Hash and clock.** `updated_at` is the database clock and is not hashed. Every load re-hashes and re-parses the row, else `integrity`. **Not enforced:** begin_send's attempt limit, the identity of the usage reporter, a cap shared across tenants, and a keyed hash (the guard is what holds the invariants).

# Increment 4: the budget expiry sweep job and saturating totals

Status: In progress. No dependency, migration, provider, credential or billable call.

**Changes.** `budget.py`: `summary()` and `reserve()` sum in plain `Decimal` under `DOMAIN_CONTEXT` (`_totals`, `_available`) and compare those exact sums with the caps, so any number of absurd reported overruns gives the typed `inference_cap` / `improvement_cap` refusal instead of `DecimalValueError` (the round-4 LOW gap of increment 3). `summary()` saturates a total beyond `UsdBudget`'s bound at ±99999999999999.999999 (`USD_BOUND`, `saturate`) and sets the new `Summary.saturated` flag; below the bound every value is exact, as before. The exact sums are public as `BudgetBook.totals()`, for arithmetic on totals. `evaluator_improvement.py`: `score()`'s cost metric now uses `totals()` instead of `summary().reserved + spent`, so a saturated summary never enters scoring (it is the only `summary()` consumer in source code that does arithmetic or comparisons; the others are tests). `budget_store.py`: `open_periods(tx)`, the session tenant's months whose book still holds a `reserved` reservation. `qw_worker/budget_sweep.py` (new): the `budget.expire_due` job (`handle`, `sweep`, `submit`), registered in `qw_worker/__main__.py` next to `optimizer.cycle`. `qw_worker/loop.py`: `HandlerFailed(..., keep_effects=True)` records the failure in the job's own transaction, so the handler's writes commit with it (default unchanged: they roll back). Tests: `test_budget.py` (two and three absurd overruns, in a shared category and in improvement; one overrun stays exact), `test_evaluator_improvement.py` (exact cost with saturating books), `test_budget_sweep.py` (new), the entry-point assertion in `test_optimizer_cycle.py`, and a stale docstring in `test_budget_store.py`.

**Undispatched reservations past their deadline.** No domain or SQL change was needed. `BudgetBook.expire_due` already releases an undispatched `reserved` reservation past its deadline (its docstring and the module docstring: `release` is for a reservation that never reached a provider), and 0052's `budget_book_step` already allows `reserved -> released` for any undispatched reservation with no sends and a null actual, at any time. The reservation stayed `reserved` forever only because nothing called `expire_due`. Since 0052 needed no change, there is no new migration.

**The job.** It runs as qw_worker, in the job tenant's transaction (the worker loop's `tenant_transaction`, so RLS scopes every read and write to that tenant). The payload is strictly `{"slot": "YYYY-MM-DDTHH"}`, else `payload_invalid` (not retried). For each month in `open_periods`, it calls `budget_store.expire_due` at the database clock, each month in its own savepoint. That covers the current month and also an earlier month: a reservation's deadline can fall up to `MAX_DEADLINE` into the next month, and sweeping only the current month would leave such a reservation `reserved` for good. This is a deliberate superset of "the current DB-clock month", and it is what the brief's two goals need. Each month is one compare-and-set step. A `stale_version` reloads and sweeps again, up to 3 times per month. A month that still fails (any store code) rolls back to its savepoint only; the job logs its real code and goes on with the remaining months. At the end, if any month failed, the job fails with the swept months kept (`keep_effects`): retryable as `database` if any failure was `stale_version`, `stale_clock` or `database`, else `budget_refused`, not retried. A retry re-sweeps only what is still due. A failure listing the months fails the job with nothing kept. A sweep changes only `status` and `actual` (`expired` with actual = reserved, or `released`); deadlines, creation times and amounts are untouched, and a test compares every other field before and after. A repeat finds nothing due and writes nothing. Priority `safety` (spec 03: reconciliation), standard pool.

**Scheduling.** The job layer has no recurring or scheduled-job mechanism (T025: `available_at` is the enqueue time; the loop's own periodic work, expiry, reaping and purges, is cross-tenant maintenance), and qw_worker cannot list tenants under RLS. So `submit(tx)` is an idempotent enqueue for `tx`'s tenant, one job per hour of the database clock (`input_revision` `expire_due:<slot>`): a repeat in the same hour returns the same job. **Gap:** nothing calls `submit` periodically yet. Until a per-tenant scheduler exists (an operator cron, or a recurring-job mechanism in T025), an overdue reservation stays `reserved` between sweeps and keeps holding its amount. That fails closed: it blocks spend and never frees it early.
