## Negative/adverse cases (SYNTHETIC)
- 40 demands against a 30-symbol cap: the 10 prioritized and the first 20 candidates by instrument id are planned; the other 10 are reported `capacity_exhausted` and listed for scheduled discovery. Rank order within a class; input-order independence.
- Feed missing from the registry, or missing derived-data rights: no stream, `rights_denied`, T021 reasons kept. Instrument outside the entitlement, insufficient latency class, no feed: reported, not dropped. The weakest sufficient feed is used first so a consolidated slot stays free for a demand that needs it.
- Starvation probe: delayed feed (cap 1, A and B) and consolidated feed (cap 1, A only); A is moved to the consolidated feed and B takes the delayed slot. A redelivered trade with a later receipt is `duplicate`, and the bar keeps the first receipt time.
- Property: no feed exceeds its cap or streams an unentitled instrument; a denied feed streams nothing; every served demand's latency need is met; served plus uncovered equals the demand multiset. Priority monotonicity: a demand uncovered for capacity on a feed never yields to a subscription held only by lower-priority demands. The property ran once locally with 5000 random examples and passed; the committed setting is 200 derandomized examples.
- Bars: hand-computed OHLCV (an ineligible 250 print excluded); a late trade and an open interval not used; pre/post rejected without `ExtendedHours` and labelled with it; early close (18:00Z) and holiday from the calendar; 7-minute bins truncated at the close; sequence gap 2→5 reported and its two intervals not built; duplicate vs conflicting sequence; a late first trade gives a version 2 (open 98) while version 1 stays visible before 13:35:55; an older-knowledge correction refused; live trades refused without rights or from another feed.
- Mutation controls (worker self-report, scratchpad-only, each reverted): 17/17 killed after one test was strengthened (before the review fixes; the augmenting step is covered by the starvation probe, which failed before the fix): cap check, rights denial, weakest-first order, rank order, latency check, entitlement check, knowledge time, gap skip, eligibility filter, open-interval filter, `visible` knowledge filter, close truncation, not-yet-known filter, gap detection, correction-order check, identical-content no-op, event-order sort.

Known limits of this increment:
- A loss at the end of an interval (the last trades before a disconnect, with no later sequence number) cannot be detected without a heartbeat or end-of-interval marker; increment 2 coverage accounting (stream windows and outages) addresses this.
- Bars have no property test; their tests are example-based with hand-computed values.
- The augmenting path fixes the case the review raised, but the plan is not proven to serve the maximum number of demands, because instruments share slots across demands. The round-2 review found, by exhaustive comparison over 3000 small random instances, one case where a lower-priority demand is served in place of a higher-priority one: freeing the slot needed a swap between two full feeds (a cycle, not a chain ending in a free slot), and a demand merging into a moved subscription of its own instrument is not searched. It needs one instrument demanded at two latency classes across feeds with different entitlements.

## Known limits
- Without markers, the increment-1 sequence-gap detection still brackets a gap by the event times of its neighbouring sequences. With markers, gaps start at the last confirmed boundary and make no ordering assumption. A marker received late leaves intervals `unconfirmed` until a rebuild after its receipt. Gaps from a build are valid for that build's `as_of`; coverage at a later time needs a rebuild.
- `retrospective` recovery may use a different feed than the live stream; it is labelled, never presented as continuous coverage (spec §6 reinitialization for an opening range is not modelled).
- Markers: sequences are assumed to start at 1; a later marker with a lower `last_sequence` than an earlier one adds nothing and is not flagged as inconsistent. Markers sharing a time are processed highest sequence first, so input order does not matter (round-2 fix; test runs both orders).
- Discovery job keys hash the batch, so a batch boundary that moves (one more or one fewer uncovered instrument) gives new keys for overlapping work; the same instrument can then be queued twice in a session. Deferred: per-instrument-per-session keys or fixed hash buckets need a handler design.
- Discovery jobs are requests only; no enqueue happens, and no handler or provider quota for the batch size exists yet.
- Bars and coverage still have only the one recovery property test; other cases are example-based.
