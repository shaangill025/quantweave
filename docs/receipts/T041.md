### Rules
See the module docstring: balanced entries (cash + cost = capital + realized + income − fees), average-cost removal to 1e-12, `virtual: Literal[True]` checked by mypy and at runtime, read-only seeding, fills only from the model feed's bars known at `as_of` and live from submission + latency, open fills at the bar start and others at the bar end, adverse slippage, strict limit penetration, worst-extreme `ambiguous` fills when order and bar collide, worse-price-first for an OCO pair in one bar, participation × volume in whole lots, and cash/unit caps.

Review fixes:
- **S1.** The sell cap and the cash cap use the ledger as of the fill stamp (entries effective at or before it). `record` refuses an entry earlier than the ledger's latest effective time (`time_order`), so a simulation against a ledger that already holds later entries raises instead of booking a back-dated fill. Fills within a bar are recorded in stamp order. The reviewer's probe (a sell at 13:20 against units bought at 15:00) is a test: no fill.
- **S2.** No fill is stamped at or after `expires_at` (`expired_in_bar`); an expiry is exclusive for both bar-start and bar-end stamps (round-2 nit N1). A gap fill at a bar start before expiry still fills. Tested with a limit expiring at 13:45.
- **Round-2 N2.** A market order that is capped by cash or units stays live for its unfilled remainder and can fill in a later bar once cash or units are available as of that bar's stamp. That is a partial-fill queue, not a rejection; it ends at `expires_at` or by cancellation.
- **N1.** `simulate` evaluates its orders together from the portfolio as it was before them. Orders already recorded in that portfolio must not be simulated again.

Oracles: every expected number is hand-computed in the test comments. Examples:
- A market buy of 100 fills at 10 + 0.05 = 10.05 with fee 1 + 100 × 0.01 = 2, leaving cash 98993.
- A participation fill splits 500/300/200 across the volumes 5000/3000/4000.
- With capital 1000, the affordable quantity is floor(999 / 10.06) = 99.
- Selling 40 realizes 398 − 402 = −4.

The property recomputes cash and units from the fill records, so it checks ledger consistency and timing, not price correctness; fill prices rest on the hand-computed tests above (beyond the range-plus-slippage bound). It checks:
- No fill comes before submission plus latency.
- No fill uses a bar that was unknown at `as_of`.
- Every fill price lies within the bar's range plus slippage.
- Cash and units are never negative at any fill time, not only at the end.
- No fill is stamped after its order's expiry (random expiries).
- cash + cost = capital + realized − fees.
