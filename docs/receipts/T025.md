**Delivery:** at-least-once. Effects happen once per (consumer, event) for effects committed in the same database transaction as the inbox row; external side effects need their own idempotency.

**Claim algorithm:** the highest priority class with ready work in the pool, then the tenant least recently served in that class (`max(claimed_at)`, never served first), then tenant id; the first unlocked ready job is locked with SKIP LOCKED. Equal weights give round-robin; priority beats fairness. Heartbeat, complete and fail need the exact lease token and an unexpired lease, else `LeaseLost`.

### Scenarios (SYNTHETIC tenants and payloads)
- Crash before commit: an exception rollback and a `pg_terminate_backend` kill after the business write, `write_outbox` and `complete` leave no outbox row, no effect and the job running with its lease.
- Crash after commit, before the ack: the effect commits through `consume` (keyed by job id), the lease expires, the job is reaped and redelivered, the inbox skips it; one effect, `succeeded` at attempt 3. Duplicate delivery on 6 connections: one effect.
- 8 concurrent claimers over 60 jobs: 60 distinct claims. Expired lease: not extendable or completable, reclaimed at attempt 2 after reaping, old token fenced, valid lease not reaped; a shorter heartbeat does not shrink the lease.
- Max attempts gives `dead_letter`; requeue starts generation 1 with attempts 0 while total_attempts (2, then 3) and `available_at` are unchanged; requeue stops after generation 10. Non-retryable failure, last-attempt lease expiry and cancel behave per the table. Direct qw_worker UPDATEs that bump generation (with attempts 0) on a running or queued job, change attempts or total_attempts alone, or move them by different amounts are refused; a requeue jumping two generations is refused; the operator requeue still works.
- Backoff of 1 h keeps the job unclaimable 30–60 min. Priority order holds with the safety tenant just served; a 1-job tenant is served within two claims against a 1000-job tenant. Pools never cross.
- Idempotency: identical repeat returns the job; other priority (including EXPERIMENT → SAFETY), payload or max_attempts raises, and the experiment job stays in its pool.
- RLS: tenant B cannot see or finish A's job; no context sees nothing; qw_app cannot run the dispatch functions, update jobs or select `lease_owner` (nor `SELECT *`); the owner role can lock but not write without a tenant context.
- Catalog: both dispatch functions are owned by qw_migrate, `prosecdef`, `proconfig = {search_path=pg_catalog}`, EXECUTE exactly qw_migrate and qw_worker. Trigger parity over all 49 pairs; `available_at` and identity frozen; history cannot shrink.

Relay semantics: one pass = one transaction holding the picked rows; marks commit together at the end, so a crash republishes every event the pass had already published (keep `limit` small). `attempts` counts failed publishes; at `max_attempts` the row gets `parked_at` and stays visible. Deadline = start-by time: no attempt starts after it (including a retry), and a running attempt is not interrupted.

Purge hazard: an inbox row is the only memory that an effect happened. A redelivery from outside the database (transport, replay) after its purge runs the effect again, so the inbox window must exceed the maximum redelivery horizon (relay default: 10 attempts, cap 30 min, so under 5 h; the 7-day minimum and 30-day default leave margin). `app.installation_bootstrap` is **not** purged: its single row is what refuses a second bootstrap, it has no expired or used state, and it cannot grow.

Semantics:
- **Relay off by default.** `Worker(publish=None)` skips the relay step; `__main__` passes none. Outbox rows (e.g. those `proposal_store` writes) stay pending: never marked published, so the outbox purge never deletes them. A real transport (network, email, broker) needs explicit owner authorisation. Round 1 shipped a recording sink as the default and called that acceptable; it was not: the sink marked events published although nothing received them, and the purge would then have deleted real events (review S1).
- Unknown kind fails closed: the attempt is recorded as retryable `no_handler` with the job backoff, so the job ends in `dead_letter` at `max_attempts` (an operator `requeue` can redrive it once a handler exists). It is never marked succeeded.
- A handler's writes and `complete` share one transaction; a failure rolls them back and records the attempt in a new transaction; `LeaseLost` (lease expired during the handler) rolls the effects back, logs a warning and leaves the running job to the reaper.
- Relay with an injected publisher (increment 2 nit): each event is picked, published and marked in its own transaction, so a crash or DB error republishes at most the one event in flight, and a row lock is held across exactly one publish.
- Stop: the flag is checked before each claim, each relay event and the purge; the unit in progress finishes and commits. After an idle tick the worker sleeps at most `idle_wait` (1 s) before noticing it.

### Gates
- **No transport and no job handler exist.** The shipped worker expires, reaps, claims (empty registry) and purges; outbox events stay pending until an owner-authorised transport is injected.
- **No heartbeat:** the worker never calls `heartbeat`, so a handler running longer than the lease (default 5 min) loses its lease and its work (rolled back, retried by the reaper). Handlers must finish well inside the lease, or a heartbeat design is needed; note that `heartbeat` opens its own transaction (`tenant_transaction`), so it cannot run on the connection that holds the handler's open transaction and needs a second connection or a handler-driven call between transactions.

### Worker kind filter: not implemented, a migration is needed
- Spec check: T025 ("dead-letter handling"), spec 03 "Durable coordination" ("an explicit dead-letter state") and R088 ("bounded leases/retries and dead-letter recovery") require a dead-letter state and recovery; `contracts/schemas/job.schema.json` says nothing about unknown kinds. Nothing requires an unknown kind to be dead-lettered. The current behaviour is not spec-mandated and should change.
- Why no migration-free fix is correct:
  - Filter in the claim query: the only cross-tenant view of `app.job` for qw_worker is the SECURITY DEFINER `app.job_pick(p_pool)` (FORCED RLS, policy `tenant_isolation`), which has no kind parameter. Adding one is a new function signature, so a migration.
  - Unclaim after the claim: `app.job_update_guard` refuses `attempts` falling outside the operator requeue (`dead_letter → queued`), and the claim has already written `claimed_at`, `heartbeat_at`, `lease_*` and `updated_at`; so the attempt cannot be given back unchanged without a migration.
  - Release before the claim's UPDATE (check `kind` after `job_pick` and commit nothing) leaves the job byte-for-byte unchanged, but `job_pick` is deterministic and `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` does not skip a row locked by the caller's own transaction, so the next call returns the same job. One unhandled job at the head of a (tenant, priority) bucket would stop the worker claiming anything else in its pool, for every tenant. A cross-tenant liveness failure is worse than the current behaviour, so it is not shipped.
- Proposed design (for the migration after 0019): `app.job_pick(p_pool app.job_pool, p_kinds text[])` (SECURITY DEFINER, `search_path = pg_catalog`), the 0006 body with `AND j.kind = ANY(p_kinds)` added to both the bucket subquery and the row query; `GRANT EXECUTE` to qw_worker only; drop or revoke the one-argument version. `qj.claim(conn, worker, pool, lease, kinds)` passes the kinds and adds `AND kind = ANY(%s)` to its UPDATE; with an empty `kinds` it returns None without a database call. `Worker._run_one` passes `tuple(self.handlers)`; the `no_handler` path stays as fail-closed defence. Tests: two workers with disjoint registries; an unknown kind stays `queued` with attempts 0 and `available_at` / `next_available_at` / `claimed_at` unchanged while a known kind behind it (same tenant and priority, and in another tenant) is claimed; an empty registry claims nothing. The fairness term (`max(claimed_at)` per tenant and priority) can stay kind-blind.
- Until then the increment 3 gate stands: a worker claims whatever kind is next in its pool, and kinds it cannot handle dead-letter after `max_attempts` (recoverable by the operator requeue). No handler is registered yet, so no job is affected today.
